A light-curve notebook

Looking for a planet’s shadow

A planet passing in front of its star briefly dims the light we observe. Follow the measurements from the Kepler mission through a simple transit search.

01 / OBSERVATIONS

Raw light curve

Each point records the star’s measured brightness at a moment in time. The measurements include both the star’s real variations and instrumental effects.

02 / CLEANING

Removing slow changes

Invalid measurements and unusually high outliers are removed, then a rolling baseline is divided out to make the typical brightness close to one. Low points are retained because a transit appears as a dip.

03 / PERIOD SEARCH

Looking for repetition

The search tests trial periods and scores how strongly a short dim interval stands out when the observations are folded on each period.

04 / REPEATING DIP

Folded transit

Folding the observations on the best period lines up repeated events. The horizontal axis is hours before or after the estimated transit midpoint.

05 / REFERENCE

Comparison with NASA

The measured period is compared with the published reference period when one is available. The radius ratio is estimated from a simplified box model, which slightly underestimates depth.
